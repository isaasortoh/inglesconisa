01 / Why it matters

It's not just what you say.

A strong update helps people make decisions. Constructive pushback helps teams catch risks without starting conflict.

Hard to act on
“We're behind. There's a problem.”

What changed? What happens next?

Easy to act on
“We're one day behind because we're waiting on approval. I'll send an updated timeline today.”

A clear update gives useful context and direction.

Today's goal: help your listener understand the situation *and* what you recommend.
02 / Your update framework

Status → Challenge → Next step

Three short parts. One useful update.

1

Status

Where are we?

“The materials are ready.”
2

Challenge

What's slowing us down?

“We're waiting on final approval.”
3

Next step

What happens now?

“I'll follow up this afternoon.”
Keep it natural: “The materials are ready. We're just waiting on approval, and I'll follow up this afternoon.”

06 / Writing challenge 1

Write the same update.

Your manager messages: “Any update on the launch materials?” Write a short Teams response.

Your goal

Use status, challenge, and next step in 1–3 sentences. Aim for clarity, not extra formality.

0 / 550 characters
Grammar shortcut: Present for status (“The draft is ready”), present progressive for a current obstacle (“We're waiting”), and a clear future action (“I'll follow up”).
“Quick update — the materials are drafted. We're waiting on final approval, and I'll check back this afternoon.”

There are several good ways to write this. Use the checklist, not an exact wording match.

07 / Constructive pushback

Respectfully disagree. Then help.

Use a simple sequence: acknowledge → concern → option.

1

Acknowledge

“I see your point, but…”

Show you heard the idea.

2

Concern

“One concern I have is…”

Explain the risk, not the person.

3

Option

“Another option might be…”

Keep the discussion productive.

Another natural phrase
“One thing we may want to consider is…”

Use it to raise a factor without making a direct rejection.

Tip: “I see your point” should be genuine. You don't have to pretend you agree; you can acknowledge the goal or reasoning instead.

09 / Speaking challenge 2

Say it diplomatically.

Coworker: “We should roll this out nationwide immediately.”

You think a smaller pilot would be safer. Give a real, natural response in your own words.

Your guide: acknowledge the idea → explain one concern → recommend an alternative. Use at least one Week 15 phrase.

10 / Writing challenge 2

Write a constructive alternative.

A teammate wants to skip a final review to save time. You think the review helps avoid mistakes.

Write 2–3 sentences: acknowledge their goal, state your concern, and suggest a next step.
0 / 600 characters
Grammar shortcut: “Might,” “could,” and “would” can soften a suggestion: “Another option might be…” or “Could we…?”
“I understand the need to move quickly. One concern I have is missing an important detail. Could we do a shorter final review today and keep the original deadline?”
11 / Leadership communication

Influence the decision.

Director: “We need to launch Friday. Is your team ready?”

Scenario: your team is mostly ready, but a final quality check remains. You recommend delaying two days to reduce risk.

Your 45-second leadership brief
  1. Lead with the current status.
  2. Name the risk respectfully.
  3. Recommend a realistic next step.
  4. Say when you'll provide an update.
Ready to record · 0:00 / 0:45
“We're close to ready, but the final quality check isn't complete. One concern I have is the risk of errors if we launch Friday. I recommend moving the launch to Monday, and I'll send a revised timeline this afternoon.”
